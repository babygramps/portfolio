Work

AI-assisted cooling research

Orbital Industries, 2026

I helped build and improve a test system for two-phase cooling research. My work connects the rig, prototype fabrication, and experimental data so we can take ideas through to physical testing.

I also developed an AI-assisted cold-plate design workflow and took generated lid designs through fabrication and testing.

More about the research

I worked with the team on changes to the rig, test procedures, and the contact surfaces that affect our measurements. Getting those details right helps us understand whether a design change actually improved cooling.

The design workflow used experimental data and basic physics models to propose new lids. We tested the resulting parts, and another engineer built on the workflow. The generated designs hadn’t outperformed our best existing lid at that stage, but we had a working path from an AI-assisted proposal to a physical experiment.

Making research hardware usable by software

Orbital Industries, 2026

AI tools need a useful record of the system they’re working with. I built tools to capture a rig’s components and connections as structured data and generate engineering diagrams from that record.

More about the engineering tools

The work grew into an engineering workbench built around DEXPI, a standard for exchanging plant engineering data. I worked on keeping the model consistent with the physical rig and giving components stable identities as the design changed.

I also worked on connecting experimental runs to the parts being tested. That context matters when an engineer or an AI tool tries to compare results or propose the next experiment.

Getting research parts made

Orbital Industries, 2026

I brought a new milling capability from equipment selection through commissioning and early cold-plate samples. I also work with suppliers on cooling surfaces that need other fabrication methods.

My part is working out what can be made, getting the tooling and drawings ready, and checking the parts before they go into a test.

More about fabrication

Setting up the shop included preparing the space, coordinating training, and writing a design guide. I also built software for tooling records and machine integration.

For supplier-made samples, I compare fabrication routes and ask how bonding or finishing might change the surface we’re trying to test. I document inspection methods and their limits so the team knows what we’ve actually checked.

A live meeting bot for CurieOS

Orbital Industries, 2026

I built an automated bot that joins live meetings, captures the audio, and transcribes the conversation as it happens. It separates speech by speaker and uses participant information to help identify who said what.

I connected it to CurieOS so the conversation becomes a record the team can use for notes, decisions, and follow-up work.

More about the bot

The work spans the meeting bot, live audio capture, streaming transcription, speaker attribution, and saving the result. I also worked on recording consent, reconnecting dropped streams, and preserving partial transcripts when a session is interrupted.

I’ve since been developing separate capture and transcription services, with live transcript APIs, evidence for speaker attribution, and a reliable handoff into CurieOS. That service work builds on the original bot.

Test benches for carbon capture

Noya, 2023–2025

I built thermal-imaging and pressure-drop test rigs, helped coordinate prototype builds, and set up a fabrication shop.

More about the thermal test bench

I built a test bench that uses three thermal cameras to watch a part as it heats. A Python application controls the temperature, records the images, and combines them into a temperature map.

Testing went from roughly 30–40 minutes per part to 5–10 minutes, and engineers no longer had to watch each run.
